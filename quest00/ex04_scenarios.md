# AI Over-reliance Problems

These are scenarios playing with the assumption I'm over-reliant on AI.

## Scenario 1

You're asked to explain a caching system design. What happens if you've always relied on AI to design systems?

I will freeze and try to remember anything to save face. Other options include either explaining that I've always used AI to design systems because that's the current market (vibe coding), or steering away from the necessity to actually explain, claiming that "in today's intelligence market, asking an AI for that answer would give a much more detailed one than the one I would be able to give, considering I'm a programmer and not a fluid and avid speaker". First option is just the truth and second option is deception and, quite frankly, straight up lies.

## Scenario 2

Production Bug: At 2 AM, you must debug code generated by AI months ago - but AI is offline. Can you fix it?

No.

## Scenario 3

New Technology: A new library is released, and AI hasn't been trained on it yet. How do you learn it?

I will try to upload it to the AI to see what it makes of it. If it can understand it, it could give me some very practical info on what it contains exactly and how to use it. Other than that I could always search for online sources of documentation or training (but I'm supposed to be over-reliant on AI, so scratch that).

## Reflection

What this whole thing means is that using AIs to do your work without auditing it at the very least, makes you incompetent when you lose access to the AI. Using it fairly on the other hand can prepare you, teach you in great speed and make you independent of it in the end. Think of it like you having a child. If you raise it to be able to do things on its own, once you're gone it will be a fully functional person whereas if you do everything for it to make things easier, once you're gone, it will be lost, needing guidance and support instead of carrying its own weight. (A bit of a grim example I must admit but to the point I think)
